# Simple sentences

Vietnamese grammar is friendlier than most: verbs never change, there are no plurals to add, and no "a" or "the". Most of the work is done by word order and a few small words. This chapter gives you the patterns for real everyday sentences.
{: .lead }

## 8.1 Word order

Vietnamese puts words in the same order as English: **who + does + what**.

[[Tôi ăn phở.]] I eat pho.

[[Chị uống trà.]] You drink tea. (to a woman a bit older)

Verbs never change form: *ăn* is "eat", "eats", "ate" and "eating". Swap the parts and listen:

<sentence-builder vi="{who} {verb} {thing}." en="{who} {verb} {thing}.">
who: Tôi = I | Bạn = You | Chúng tôi = We
verb: ăn = eat | thích = like | mua = buy
thing: phở = pho | xoài = mangoes | bánh mì = bánh mì
</sentence-builder>

### When did it happen?

Since the verb never changes, time comes from context or from a small word in front of the verb: **đã** (already happened), **đang** (happening now) or **sẽ** (will happen).

<sentence-builder vi="Tôi {time} ăn." en="{time}">
time: đã = I ate. | đang = I'm eating. | sẽ = I'll eat.
</sentence-builder>

In everyday speech these are often left out when the time is obvious: *Hôm qua tôi ăn phở* is just "yesterday I eat pho", and everyone understands.

<div class="south" markdown="1">
**Chúng tôi** is the standard "we". Among friends, Southerners often say **tụi mình** or **tụi tui** instead.
</div>

## 8.2 "Is": là, or nothing at all

**là** links two *things* (nouns): "A is B".

[[Tôi là sinh viên.]] I'm a student.

[[Đây là nhà tôi.]] This is my house.

But descriptions (adjectives) **don't use là**. The adjective follows directly, like a verb:

[[Tôi mệt.]] I'm tired. (literally "I tired")

[[Phở ngon.]] The pho is delicious.

<div class="warn" markdown="1">
*Tôi là mệt* is a classic beginner mistake. Use **là** only before a noun.
</div>

To say "very", put **rất** before the adjective. In conversation you'll more often hear **quá** ("so…!") or the Southern **lắm** after it:

[[Phở rất ngon.]] · [[Ngon quá!]] · [[Đẹp lắm!]]

<sentence-builder vi="{thing} rất {adj}." en="The {thing} is very {adj}.">
thing: Phở = pho | Trà = tea | Cà phê = coffee
adj: ngon = good | rẻ = cheap | mắc = expensive | nóng = hot
</sentence-builder>

## 8.3 Saying no

Put **không** ("not") before the verb or adjective:

[[Tôi không hiểu.]] I don't understand.

[[Tôi không ăn thịt.]] I don't eat meat.

<sentence-builder vi="Tôi {how} {adj}." en="I'm {how} {adj}.">
how: không = not | hơi = a bit | rất = very
adj: mệt = tired | đói = hungry | vui = happy | bận = busy
</sentence-builder>

For a noun, say **không phải là** ("is not"):

[[Tôi không phải là người Mỹ.]] I'm not American.

And for "not yet", use **chưa**:

[[Tôi chưa ăn.]] I haven't eaten yet.

## 8.4 Yes/no questions

To ask a yes/no question, add **không** at the end. Often **có** goes before the verb too:

[[Anh khỏe không?]] Are you well?

[[Chị có thích phở không?]] Do you like pho?

<sentence-builder vi="{you} {adj} không?" en="Are you {adj}? (to {you})">
you: Anh = a man a bit older | Chị = a woman a bit older | Em = someone younger
adj: khỏe = well | mệt = tired | đói = hungry
</sentence-builder>

<sentence-builder vi="{you} có {verb} không?" en="Do you {verb}? (to {you})">
you: Anh = a man a bit older | Chị = a woman a bit older | Em = someone younger
verb: thích phở = like pho | uống cà phê = drink coffee | ăn cay = eat spicy food
</sentence-builder>

### Answering

There's no single word for "yes". Answer with **có** (yes) or **không** (no), or simply repeat the verb or adjective. Add **dạ** to be polite.

| Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| [[Anh có thích phở không?]] | [[Dạ, thích.]] | [[Dạ, không thích.]] |
| [[Em có mệt không?]] | [[Dạ, có.]] | [[Dạ, không.]] |

### "…yet?" and "…right?"

End with **chưa** to ask whether something has happened yet. The answers are **rồi** (already) or **chưa** (not yet). *Ăn cơm chưa?* ("Eaten yet?") is also a friendly greeting.

[[Anh ăn cơm chưa?]] Have you eaten yet? · [[Dạ, rồi.]] Yes, I have. · [[Dạ, chưa.]] Not yet.

End with **phải không** ("right?") to check something you think is true:

[[Chị là người Úc, phải không?]] You're Australian, right?

## 8.5 Useful verbs

| Vietnamese | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| [[có]] | to have; there is | [[Ở đây có wifi không?]] Is there wifi here? |
| [[đi]] | to go | [[Tôi đi chợ.]] I'm going to the market. |
| [[muốn]] | to want | [[Tôi muốn uống nước.]] I want to drink some water. |
| [[biết]] | to know | [[Tôi không biết.]] I don't know. |
| [[hiểu]] | to understand | [[Anh hiểu không?]] Do you understand? |
| [[nói]] | to speak | [[Tôi nói tiếng Việt một chút.]] I speak a little Vietnamese. |
| [[làm]] | to do; to work | [[Chị làm gì?]] What do you do? |

Two lifesavers:

[[Xin lỗi, tôi không hiểu.]] Sorry, I don't understand.

[[Anh nói chậm được không?]] Could you speak slowly?

<sentence-builder vi="Tôi muốn {verb}." en="I want to {verb}.">
verb: đi = go | ăn = eat | uống cà phê = drink coffee | học tiếng Việt = learn Vietnamese | về nhà = go home
</sentence-builder>

## 8.6 Dialogue: at a café

Ordering is one pattern: **Cho** ("give") + *me* + the thing. *Me* is your word for yourself, so it depends on who's serving you.

<sentence-builder vi="Cho {me} một {item}." en="One {item}, please.">
me: anh = a man, to a younger server | chị = a woman, to a younger server | con = to someone your parents' age
item: ly cà phê sữa đá = iced milk coffee | ly trà đá = iced tea | tô phở = bowl of pho | ổ bánh mì = bánh mì
</sentence-builder>

Mark orders an iced coffee in Saigon. The server is younger than him.

<div class="dialogue" markdown="1">
- **Mark:** [[Em ơi!]] *Excuse me!*
- **Server:** [[Dạ, anh uống gì ạ?]] *What would you like to drink?*
- **Mark:** [[Cho anh một ly cà phê sữa đá.]] *An iced milk coffee, please.*
- **Server:** [[Dạ. Anh có ăn gì không?]] *Anything to eat?*
- **Mark:** [[Không, cảm ơn em. Ở đây có wifi không?]] *No, thanks. Is there wifi here?*
- **Server:** [[Dạ, có.]] *Yes, there is.*
- **Mark:** [[Em ơi, tính tiền!]] *Excuse me, the bill please!*
- **Server:** [[Dạ, hai mươi lăm ngàn ạ.]] *That's 25,000.*
- **Mark:** [[Gửi em. Cảm ơn nha!]] *Here you go. Thanks!*
</div>

New words: **ly** (glass), **cà phê sữa đá** (iced coffee with condensed milk, a Saigon classic), **tính tiền** (to pay the bill), **ở đây** (here).

<div class="south" markdown="1">
**ly**, **tô** and **ổ** (for a glass, a bowl and a loaf of bread) are the Southern words. In the North you'll hear **cốc** for a glass and **bát** for a bowl.
</div>

## 8.7 Practice

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Fix the mistake: <em>Tôi là mệt.</em></summary>

[[Tôi mệt.]] Adjectives don't take *là*.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Make it a question: <em>Anh khỏe.</em></summary>

[[Anh khỏe không?]] Add *không* at the end.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Say: "I'm not American, I'm Australian."</summary>

[[Tôi không phải là người Mỹ, tôi là người Úc.]]
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Say: "Have you eaten yet?" (to a woman a bit older)</summary>

[[Chị ăn cơm chưa?]]
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Order a bowl of pho (you're a woman, the server is younger)</summary>

[[Cho chị một tô phở.]]
</details>
