These are paraphrased notes for writing our chapters, not copied text. **The source teaches a standard/Northern pronunciation** (its audio is Northern), so every pronunciation point below is checked against the Southern accent. ⚠️ marks the places where we differ or disagree.

## Ch 1: How Vietnamese works

- Letters have **two naming systems**: a spelling-out-loud set borrowed from French (b "bê", c "xê", d "dê", đ "đê", g "giê", h "hát", l "e-lờ", m "em-mờ", n "en-nờ", q "quy", r "e-rờ", s "ét-xì", x "ích-xì") and a phonics set (bờ, cờ, dờ…). We teach the phonics names and mention the other set.
- Their alphabet example words are mostly the same everyday words we use (ba, ăn, bà, gà, hai, kem, thư…).

## Ch 2: Vowels

**Single vowels in contrast groups** (their teaching order; ours should match):
- **a / ă / â**: a is open and long; ă is a short a; â is a short, relaxed "uh". ă and â never end a syllable: a consonant (or y/u) always follows.
  Minimal sets: an / ăn / ân · bán / bắn / bấn · cát / cắt / cất · mạn / mặn / mận
- **e / ê**: e is open (like *bed*); ê is closer, like *say* without the glide. Sets: me / mê · bèn / bền · hét / hết · lẽ / lễ
- **o / ô / ơ**: o has the mouth open and lips rounded; ô has the lips rounded and pushed forward; ơ is completely relaxed. Sets: ho / hô / hơ · bó / bố / bớ · sót / sốt / sớt · đòn / đồn / đờn
  - ⚠️ Their English comparisons (ô "as in fall") are misleading. Use ours: o ≈ "aw" in *law*, ô ≈ "oh" with no glide, ơ ≈ "uh" in *fur* without the r.
- **u / ư**: u has rounded, pushed-forward lips; ư is the same tongue position with the lips spread (try smiling while saying "oo"; clenching the teeth helps). Sets: tu / tư · chú / chứ · thùng / thừng · cũ / cữ
  - ⚠️ "ư as in *pull*" is misleading. ư has no English match.
- **i / y**: the same sound. i is "i ngắn" (short i) and y is "i dài" (long y). Sets: kí / kỵ, im / ỉn / ích

**Two-vowel combinations:**
- Easy ones with English matches: ai (≈ *high*), ao (≈ *how*), ây (≈ *day*), âu (≈ *show*), oi (≈ *boy*), uy (≈ *we*), oa (≈ the "wa" of *Washington*), oe (≈ *when*).
- **ao vs au**: au closes to "u" quickly (the a is short); ao glides slowly.
- **ai vs ay**: ay starts from a short a and is more relaxed; ai starts wide and tense.
- Mouth glides: ôi (lips forward, then spread), ơi (relaxed, then tongue up to i), êu (ê, then round to u), ui (round, then spread), ưi, ưu.
- ⚠️ In the North, ưu is often said like "iu". In the South keep ư → u.

**Spelling twins (one sound, two spellings):**
- **ia / iê / yê**: *ia* at the end of a syllable (mía, chia); *iê* when a consonant follows and one comes before (tiếng, miền); *yê* when nothing comes before (yên, yêu).
- **ua / uô**: *ua* when nothing follows (mua, của); *uô* when a consonant follows (muốn, luộc).
- **ưa / ươ**: *ưa* when nothing follows (mưa, vừa); *ươ* when a consonant follows (đường, hướng).
- **uâ** is wider than ua/uô (a "wow"-sized opening) but rarely matters.
- **oă** always takes a final consonant (hoặc, xoắn).

**Three-vowel combinations** (⚠️ our outline doesn't cover these yet; add a section to Ch 2). Teach each as a vowel plus a pair:
- iêu / yêu = iê + u (yêu, chiều). Southern: often just "iu".
- oai = o + ai (xoài, ngoại); oay = o + ay (xoay). The difference is the same as ai vs ay.
- uôi = uô + i (tuổi, chuối). Southern: often "ui".
- uya / uyê = u + ia (khuya is the only uya; thuyền, chuyện).
- ươi = ươ + i (người, mười). Southern: often "ưi".
- ươu = ươ + u (rượu, hươu). Southern: often "ưu". Northern: "iêu".

## Ch 3: Tones

- Six tones, five marks. *Thanh* = the tone and *dấu* = the mark, so ngang is "thanh ngang" with no "dấu".
- **Where the tone mark goes** (great for 3.4):
  1. One vowel: on that vowel (mã, mình, bạn).
  2. Two or three vowels, nothing after: on the second-to-last vowel (hòa, hai, kiểu, mỗi).
  3. Two or three vowels plus a final consonant: on the last vowel (tiếng, Việt, Nguyễn).
  4. (Our addition) A vowel with a vowel mark (ă â ê ô ơ ư) takes priority: người, thuở, chuyện. The *u* of *qu* and the *i* of *gi* count as part of the consonant (quả, giờ).
  5. (Our addition) Both hòa and hoà are seen; the old style (hòa) is still most common.
- Literal tone names, nice for memory: ngang "flat", sắc "sharp", huyền "grave/deep", hỏi "question", ngã "tumbling", nặng "heavy".
- **How to make each tone** (adapt for the South):
  - sắc: rising like a surprised "What?!", with air flowing freely.
  - ngang: hold one steady pitch and don't drop at the end.
  - huyền: drop gently, like the end of an English sentence. The easiest one for English speakers.
  - hỏi: sounds unsure or questioning ("hỏi" means "ask").
  - ⚠️ ngã: the source describes the Northern "broken"/creaky rise. **In the South, ngã = hỏi.** Don't teach the break.
  - ⚠️ nặng: the source describes the Northern sharp cut-off in the throat. **Southern nặng** is low with a dip and slight rise, without the hard stop. Mention the Northern version only as "what you'll hear in Hà Nội".
- **Confusing pairs to drill**: the source uses sắc vs ngã, ngang vs huyền, hỏi vs nặng (Northern pairs). For the South, use **ngang vs huyền**, **sắc vs hỏi/ngã** (both rise; hỏi dips first) and **hỏi/ngã vs nặng** (both low).
- Tone sets with meanings: ma (ghost) / má (mom, cheek) / mà (but) / mả (grave) / mã (horse, appearance) / mạ (rice seedling). ba (three, dad) / bà (grandma, Mrs.) / bá (aunt; super) / bả (poison, bait) / bã (residue) / bạ (indiscriminate).
- Their example words per tone are good everyday words for drills:
  - sắc: nói, sáng, tốt, tối, đúng, phút
  - ngã: chữ, sẽ, Mỹ, rưỡi, bữa, mỗi
  - ngang: tên, anh, trưa, đêm, chơi
  - huyền: chào, người, giờ, bài, đường
  - hỏi: hỏi, phải, của, chỉ
  - nặng: một, nặng, mệt, chị, mẹ

## Ch 4: Consonants

- 17 single consonants; **đ** is the only one with no English twin.
- c, k, p, t have **no puff of air** (unaspirated), like the t in *stop*, not *top*.
- ⚠️ **th** is the aspirated one (like English *t* in *top*). The source's description is confusing. Use the hand-in-front-of-mouth test: you feel air on *th*, not on *t*.
- **Southern specifics** (the source agrees):
  - d → "y" (da, dâu, dịch)
  - gi → "y" (giỏi, giàu)
  - v → "y" or a soft v (casual speech)
  - r stays an r-sound in the South. It merges with d/gi in the North.
  - s stays a "sh"-like sound in the South. It merges with x in the North.
  - tr stays distinct from ch in the South. It merges with ch in the North.
- **ch** (start of a word): softer than English *ch*, with no burst. **tr**: closer to English *ch* in the South (tongue curled back).
- **kh**: like a gentle snore or the *ch* in Scottish *loch*. Never a hard k.
- **nh**: flat tongue against the roof of the mouth, like *ñ* in Spanish *señor*.
- **ng / ngh** at the start of a word: say "sing-along" and drop the "si-". Practise: nga, ngủ, người.
- **ph** = f; **qu** = kw (Southern: often closer to "w").
- **Spelling rules:**
  - gh, ngh, k before **i, e, ê**
  - g, ng, c everywhere else
  - q always comes with u (qu)

## Ch 5: Final sounds

- Final consonants are **not released**: the mouth closes into position and stops.
  - -c / -ng: back of the tongue up, throat closed
  - -m / -p: lips sealed
  - -n / -t: tongue tip on the upper teeth/gum
  - -nh / -ch: flat tongue on the roof of the mouth, no release
- ⚠️ The source doesn't cover **Southern mergers** (-n/-ng, -t/-c, -nh → -n, -ch → -t, lips closing in -ông/-ôc). These come from our own outline.

## Ch 6: Greetings & introductions

- **chào** on its own is the everyday hello. **xin chào** is formal or textbook. **chào** also works as goodbye, but **xin chào** doesn't. **tạm biệt** = goodbye (formal).
- Greeting pattern: **[me] + chào + [you]**, using kinship words: *Em chào chị*, *Con chào ba má*, *Chào bạn*.
- Politeness particles at the end of a sentence:
  - **ạ** shows respect to someone older or senior (*Em chào cô ạ*)
  - **nha** is Southern and friendly or softening (*Chào các bạn nha*)
  - **nhé / nhá** are the Northern equivalents
- Pronouns:
  - teacher = thầy (m) / cô (f), and you call yourself em (or con)
  - classmates = bạn, and you call yourself tôi / **tui** (Southern, casual) / mình
- Grammar: **A là B** (Tôi là Nam). **gì** = "what" at the end of a sentence (Tên bạn là gì? = "Your name is what?").
