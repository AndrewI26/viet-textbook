# Vowels

Vietnamese vowels are *pure*: your mouth takes one shape and holds it. English vowels slide. Say "no" slowly and you'll hear it end in a "w". Vietnamese doesn't do that, so holding still is the first habit to learn.
{: .lead }

Three things make each vowel different:

1. **How open your mouth is**: from nearly closed (*i*, *u*) to wide open (*a*).
2. **Your lips**: rounded and pushed forward (*u*, *ô*, *o*) or spread (*i*, *ê*, *ư*).
3. **Your tongue**: forward in the mouth (*i*, *ê*, *e*) or pulled back (*u*, *ô*, *o*, *ư*, *ơ*).

## 2.1 The single vowels

There are twelve vowel letters but only eleven sounds, because **i** and **y** sound the same.

| Vowel | Sounds like | Your mouth | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[a]] | "ah" in *father*, long | wide open, tongue relaxed | [[ba]] three; dad |
| ă | a short, clipped "ah" | like *a*, but cut short | [[ăn]] to eat |
| â | a short "uh", as in *but* | half open, relaxed | [[ấm]] warm |
| [[e]] | "e" in *bed*, a little more open | jaw low, lips spread | [[em]] younger sibling |
| [[ê]] | "ay" in *say*, without the "-ee" at the end | half closed, lips spread | [[tên]] name |
| [[i]] / y | "ee" in *see* | nearly closed, lips spread like a smile | [[đi]] to go |
| [[o]] | "aw" in *law* | open, lips loosely rounded | [[có]] to have |
| [[ô]] | "oh", but without the "-w" at the end | half closed, lips rounded and pushed forward | [[cô]] aunt; Miss |
| [[ơ]] | "uh" in *her*, without the r | half open, everything relaxed | [[cơm]] cooked rice |
| [[u]] | "oo" in *food* | nearly closed, lips rounded and pushed forward | [[bún]] rice noodles |
| [[ư]] | "oo" with your lips spread | nearly closed, lips spread like a smile | [[thư]] letter |

Two more things to know:

- **ă** and **â** are always short, and something always follows them: a consonant (*ăn*, *ấm*) or a short *u* or *y* (*sâu*, *cây*). They never end a syllable.
- **i** and **y** are the same sound. Which one is written depends on the word, so learn the spelling with each word: *Mỹ*, *quý*, *đi*, *chị*.

Listen to the vowels with the same consonant in front. Then use "Quiz me" to test your ear.

<word-drill labels="a, ê, o, ô, ơ, u, ư">
[[ta]] [[tê]] [[to]] [[tô]] [[tơ]] [[tu]] [[tư]]
</word-drill>

## 2.2 Vowels that are easy to mix up

Most of the trouble comes from four groups. In each one, change only one thing about your mouth.

### a, ă and â

**a** is long and open. **ă** is the same sound, just shorter. **â** is short too, but more relaxed, like the "u" in *but*.

<word-drill labels="sand, to cut, to put away">
[[cát]] [[cắt]] [[cất]]
</word-drill>

<minimal-pair labels="salty, wax apple">
[[mặn]] [[mận]]
</minimal-pair>

<div class="south" markdown="1">
In the South, [[mận]] is a wax apple (rose apple). In the North the same word means plum. Also note that many Southern speakers end words like [[ăn]] with an *-ng* sound ("ăng"). Chapter 5 explains why.
</div>

### e and ê

**e** is open, with your jaw low, like *bed*. **ê** is more closed, like *say* without the glide at the end.

<minimal-pair labels="to scream, finished">
[[hét]] [[hết]]
</minimal-pair>

<minimal-pair labels="younger sibling, soft">
[[em]] [[êm]]
</minimal-pair>

### o, ô and ơ

**o** is open, with lips loosely rounded ("aw"). **ô** has the lips tightly rounded and pushed forward ("oh"). **ơ** has no rounding at all: relax everything and say "uh".

<word-drill labels="big, bowl, silk">
[[to]] [[tô]] [[tơ]]
</word-drill>

<div class="warn" markdown="1">
English "oh" slides into a "w": *no-w*, *go-w*. Vietnamese **ô** holds still. Keep your lips frozen until the sound ends.
</div>

### u and ư

**u** is "oo" with rounded lips. **ư** uses the same tongue position, but with your lips spread wide.

To find **ư**: say "oooo", then slowly pull your lips into a smile without moving your tongue. The sound that comes out is **ư**.

<minimal-pair labels="autumn, letter">
[[thu]] [[thư]]
</minimal-pair>

<minimal-pair labels="uncle, of course">
[[chú]] [[chứ]]
</minimal-pair>

<div class="tip" markdown="1">
**ư** is the vowel English speakers find strangest. Exaggerate the smile at first. You can relax it once your ear knows the sound.
</div>

## 2.3 Two vowels together

Many syllables glide from one vowel to another. The first vowel is usually the main one and the second is a quick finish. The exceptions are **oa**, **oe**, **uy** and **uê**, where the first vowel is a quick "w".

| Spelling | Sounds like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ai | "eye" | [[hai]] two |
| ay | a shorter "eye" | [[tay]] hand |
| ao | "ow" in *how* | [[chào]] hello |
| au | a short "ah", closing fast to "oo" | [[sau]] after |
| âu | "oh" in *go*, with the glide | [[đâu]] where |
| ây | "ay" in *day* | [[đây]] here |
| eo | "eh" sliding to "aw" | [[mèo]] cat |
| êu | "ay" sliding to "oo" | [[nếu]] if |
| iu | "ee-oo", as in *few* | [[xíu]] tiny |
| oi | "oy" in *boy* | [[nói]] to speak |
| ôi | "oh-ee" | [[tôi]] I, me |
| ơi | "uh-ee" | [[chơi]] to play |
| ui | "oo-ee" | [[vui]] happy |
| ưi | "ư-ee" | [[ngửi]] to smell |
| ưu | "ư-oo" | [[cứu]] to save |
| oa | "wa" | [[hoa]] flower |
| oe | "weh" | [[khỏe]] healthy, well |
| uy | "wee" | [[tuy]] although |
| uê | "way" | [[quê]] hometown |

The tricky pairs are **ai / ay** and **ao / au**. In **ay** and **au**, the *a* is short and the second vowel comes quickly.

<minimal-pair labels="ear, hand">
[[tai]] [[tay]]
</minimal-pair>

<minimal-pair labels="rice porridge, grandchild">
[[cháo]] [[cháu]]
</minimal-pair>

The *o* group from 2.2 comes back here with an *-i* on the end:

<word-drill labels="to speak, to connect, place">
[[nói]] [[nối]] [[nơi]]
</word-drill>

## 2.4 One sound, two spellings

Three glides are spelled two ways, depending on what comes after them. The sound doesn't change.

| Sound | Nothing after it | A consonant after it |
|---|---|---|
| "ee-uh" | **ia**: [[mía]] sugarcane, [[chia]] to share | **iê**: [[tiền]] money, [[tiếng]] language |
| "oo-uh" | **ua**: [[mua]] to buy, [[của]] of | **uô**: [[muốn]] to want, [[uống]] to drink |
| "ư-uh" | **ưa**: [[mưa]] rain, [[sữa]] milk | **ươ**: [[nước]] water, [[đường]] road |

One more rule: when nothing comes *before* "ee-uh", it's written **yê**: [[yêu]] (to love), [[yên]] (calm).

The second half of each glide is a short, soft "uh". Don't say it as a full second syllable: [[mía]] is one quick syllable, not "mee-ah".

<minimal-pair labels="to buy, rain">
[[mua]] [[mưa]]
</minimal-pair>

<div class="south" markdown="1">
In everyday Southern speech, some of these glides shrink to a single vowel before certain endings: [[muốn]] often sounds like "mún", and [[kiếm]] (to look for) like "kím". You'll still write them the long way.
</div>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Why is it <em>mía</em> but <em>tiếng</em>?</summary>

Nothing follows the glide in *mía*, so it's spelled **ia**. In *tiếng* the consonant *ng* follows, so it's **iê**.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>How do you spell "to love"?</summary>

**yêu**. Nothing comes before the glide, so it starts with **y**: [[yêu]].
</details>

## 2.5 Three vowels together

A few syllables have three vowels. Don't panic: each one is a vowel you know plus a pair you know.

| Spelling | Break it down | Example | Often in the South |
|---|---|---|---|
| iêu / yêu | iê + u | [[nhiều]] much, many | "nhìu" |
| oai | o + ai | [[xoài]] mango | |
| oay | o + ay | [[xoay]] to turn | |
| uôi | uô + i | [[chuối]] banana | "chúi" |
| uyê | u + yê | [[chuyện]] story | |
| ươi | ươ + i | [[người]] person | "ngừi" |
| ươu | ươ + u | [[rượu]] alcohol | "rựu" |

Some more to try: [[chiều]] (afternoon), [[tuổi]] (age), [[mười]] (ten), [[cười]] (to laugh), [[thuyền]] (boat), and [[khuya]] (late at night), the only word spelled *uya*.

<div class="south" markdown="1">
Southern speakers often drop the middle vowel in relaxed speech, as the table shows. It's good to *recognise* both versions, but say the full version until you're comfortable. It's always understood.
</div>
