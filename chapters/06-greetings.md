# Greetings and introductions

In Vietnamese you can't say "hello", or even "I" and "you", without first deciding how you're related to the person you're talking to. This chapter teaches that system, then how to greet people, introduce yourself, and be polite.
{: .lead }

## 6.1 Saying hello

The everyday word is **chào**. It means both "hello" and "goodbye". You almost always say *who* you're greeting, using the word for that person (see 6.2):

| Vietnamese | When |
|---|---|
| [[Chào anh!]] | Hi! (to a man a bit older than you) |
| [[Chào chị!]] | Hi! (to a woman a bit older than you) |
| [[Chào em!]] | Hi! (to someone younger) |
| [[Chào bạn!]] | Hi! (to someone your age) |
| [[Xin chào!]] | Hello! (formal: to a group, a stranger, or on a sign) |

**Xin chào** is the textbook "hello", but in everyday life it sounds stiff. A plain **chào** plus the person is much more natural.

### Being extra polite

To someone older, or someone you want to show respect to, put *yourself* first and add **ạ** at the end:

[[Em chào anh ạ.]] Hello (from a younger person, politely).

[[Con chào cô ạ.]] Hello (to a woman about your parents' age).

### Saying goodbye

| Vietnamese | English |
|---|---|
| [[Chào anh nha!]] | Bye! (*nha* makes it warm and friendly, very Southern) |
| [[Tạm biệt!]] | Goodbye. |
| [[Hẹn gặp lại!]] | See you again! |

## 6.2 Who's who: words for "I" and "you"

Vietnamese uses **family words** for "I" and "you". You choose them by comparing your age (and the other person's gender). Calling a stranger "older brother" isn't strange at all: it's how everyone talks.

| Call them… | It means | Use it for | And call yourself |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[anh]] | older brother | a man a bit older than you | [[em]] |
| [[chị]] | older sister | a woman a bit older than you | [[em]] |
| [[em]] | younger sibling | anyone younger than you | [[anh]] or [[chị]] |
| [[cô]] | aunt | a woman about your parents' age; a female teacher | [[con]] |
| [[chú]] | uncle | a man about your parents' age | [[con]] |
| [[bà]] | grandmother | an older woman | [[con]] |
| [[ông]] | grandfather | an older man | [[con]] |
| [[bạn]] | friend | someone your age; anyone, in neutral settings | [[tôi]], [[tui]], [[mình]] |

A few more things to know:

- **[[tôi]]** is the neutral "I", polite with strangers. **[[tui]]** is the relaxed Southern version, and **[[mình]]** is friendly "I" among friends.
- **[[con]]** (child) is what Southerners call themselves when talking to anyone their parents' age or older, from a shopkeeper to a friend's mother.
- For "you all", add **các**: [[các anh]], [[các chị]], [[các bạn]].

<div class="tip" markdown="1">
**Not sure?** For an adult who isn't elderly, use [[anh]] for a man and [[chị]] for a woman, and call yourself [[em]]. It's polite, slightly flattering, and almost never wrong.
</div>

### He and she

Add **ấy** ("that") for he or she: [[anh ấy]] (he, a young man), [[chị ấy]] (she, a young woman), [[ông ấy]] (he, an older man).

<div class="south" markdown="1">
In the South, these often shrink into one word with the *hỏi* tone: [[ảnh]] (anh ấy), [[chỉ]] (chị ấy), [[ổng]] (ông ấy), [[bả]] (bà ấy), [[cổ]] (cô ấy). You'll hear this all the time in Saigon.
</div>

## 6.3 Introducing yourself

Two patterns cover most introductions.

**Name:** *Tôi tên là ___*, "I name is ___". In relaxed speech you can drop *là*.

[[Tôi tên là Lan.]] My name is Lan.

[[Em tên Minh.]] I'm Minh. (to someone older, relaxed)

**Who you are:** *A là B* links two things: "A is B".

[[Tôi là người Mỹ.]] I'm American. (*người* = person, *Mỹ* = America)

[[Đây là anh Minh.]] This is Minh. (*đây* = this, here)

Some countries: [[Mỹ]] (America), [[Anh]] (Britain), [[Úc]] (Australia), [[Pháp]] (France), [[Việt Nam]] (Vietnam). Put **người** in front for the people: [[người Úc]] (an Australian).

<div class="tip" markdown="1">
**là** only links two *things*. "I am happy" doesn't use *là* at all. Chapter 8 covers that.
</div>

Then finish with [[Rất vui được gặp anh.]] Nice to meet you. (Swap *anh* for whoever you're talking to.)

## 6.4 Asking someone's name

In a Vietnamese question, **gì** ("what") sits where the answer goes, at the end:

[[Anh tên gì?]] What's your name? (literally "you name what?")

[[Em tên gì?]] What's your name? (to someone younger)

**Where are they from?** *nước* = country, *nào* = which:

[[Chị là người nước nào?]] Where are you from? (literally "you are a person of which country?")

**And you?** Turn the question around with **còn**:

[[Còn anh?]] And you?

## 6.5 Polite words

| Vietnamese | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| [[dạ]] | yes (polite) | The Southern polite *yes*. Also how you answer when someone calls you. |
| [[ạ]] | (respect) | Added at the end of a sentence to someone older. |
| [[cảm ơn]] | thank you | Add the person: [[Cảm ơn chị.]] Often said *cám ơn* in the South. |
| [[không có gì]] | you're welcome | Literally "it's nothing". |
| [[xin lỗi]] | sorry; excuse me | |
| [[không sao]] | it's okay, no problem | |
| [[nha]] | (friendly) | Southern softener at the end: [[Cảm ơn nha!]] |

<div class="south" markdown="1">
Southerners say [[dạ]] a lot: to say yes, to show they're listening, and to answer when called. In the North you'll hear **vâng** instead.
</div>

## 6.6 Dialogues

**At a café.** Lan (25) meets Mark (30), an American.

<div class="dialogue" markdown="1">
- **Lan:** [[Xin chào!]] *Hello!*
- **Mark:** [[Chào em! Em tên gì?]] *Hi! What's your name?*
- **Lan:** [[Dạ, em tên Lan. Còn anh?]] *I'm Lan. And you?*
- **Mark:** [[Anh tên Mark. Anh là người Mỹ.]] *I'm Mark. I'm American.*
- **Lan:** [[Rất vui được gặp anh!]] *Nice to meet you!*
- **Mark:** [[Anh cũng vậy. Cảm ơn em nha!]] *Same here. Thanks!*
- **Lan:** [[Dạ, không có gì. Tạm biệt anh!]] *You're welcome. Bye!*
</div>

Lan is younger, so she calls herself **em** and calls Mark **anh**. Mark does the reverse.

**At a market stall.** Mark greets the owner, a woman about his mother's age.

<div class="dialogue" markdown="1">
- **Mark:** [[Con chào cô ạ!]] *Hello!*
- **Cô:** [[Chào con! Con tên gì?]] *Hi! What's your name?*
- **Mark:** [[Dạ, con tên Mark.]] *I'm Mark.*
- **Cô:** [[Con là người nước nào?]] *Where are you from?*
- **Mark:** [[Dạ, con là người Mỹ.]] *I'm American.*
</div>

Now Mark is the younger one, so he becomes **con** and calls her **cô**.

## 6.7 Practice

Decide what to call each person, and what to call yourself.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>A man a few years older than you</summary>

**anh**, and you're **em**: [[Em chào anh.]]
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>A woman about your mother's age</summary>

**cô**, and you're **con**: [[Con chào cô ạ.]]
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>A new classmate your own age</summary>

**bạn**, and you're **tôi**, **tui** or **mình**: [[Chào bạn!]]
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>A child</summary>

**em**, and you're **anh** (if you're a man) or **chị** (if you're a woman): [[Chào em!]]
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Say: "Thank you" to a woman a bit older than you</summary>

[[Cảm ơn chị.]]
</details>

*Tôi tên là …, tôi là người Úc.* For example: [[Tôi tên là Sam. Tôi là người Úc.]]
</details>
